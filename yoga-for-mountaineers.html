Why high-altitude climbing is so hard

On the world's highest peaks, such as Mount Everest, Kangchenjunga, Lhotse, Makalu and Manaslu, the higher you go, the harder it gets. The most common problem at height is less oxygen, which makes climbing more difficult and can lead to hypoxia.

Symptoms can range from mild to severe:

  • Confusion
  • Restlessness
  • Breathlessness
  • Tachycardia (increased heart rate)
  • Bluish skin

Some climbers are left with permanent disabilities, and thousands have lost their lives to severe hypoxia.

Why yoga works for climbers

Maharshi Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga has eight limbs: Yama, Niyama, Asan, Pranayam, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyan and Samadhi. For climbers, the most important are asanas, pranayama and meditation. Starting with any one brings the others along.

Asanas

Work on the muscles and joints, and on the mind as well.

Pranayama

Works directly on the breath and the whole respiratory system, and on the mind too.

Dhyan (meditation)

Has a tremendous effect on the mind.
